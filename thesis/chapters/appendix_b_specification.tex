\subsection{Parameter Register}\label{app:register}

Table~\ref{tab:param-register} gives every parameter with its value, provenance and sweep range.
It is generated from the parameter declarations in \texttt{simulation/config.py} by
\texttt{notebooks/scripts/generate\_param\_register.py}.

Four groups of entries call for caution:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The fitted and derived parameters.} Payment friction and arrears persistence
    (Submodel~6) are fitted jointly to the five \ac{CCMR} arrears bands. The separation rate
    (Submodel~1) is derived from the stable-employment condition, and the write-off rate
    (Submodel~7) from the stationarity of the \ac{CCMR} 120+ stock; neither is fitted
    (Section~\ref{sec:lim-calibration}). Which households start in arrears, and the 120+ ages,
    are assumed.
    \item \textbf{The uncited rules.} The shortfall borrowing amount (Submodel~4), \ac{BNPL} relief
    of a shortfall and its cap (Submodel~5), the minimum-payment fraction (Submodel~6), arrears persistence
    (Submodel~6), non-performing default with no collections (Submodel~7) and platform routing (Submodel~13)
    have no literature anchor. Each is varied in at least one arm. Six assumptions are not
    varied: purchase-size dispersion; the order cap, which binds on 0.44\% of platform requests
    at $\beta=0$; the discretionary floor of zero (Submodel~2); the payment order, food and rent
    first, then \ac{BNPL}, then traditional debt (Section~\ref{sec:sequence}); the cut of a
    want-driven order to what the household can pay at checkout; and refusal at the fee cap
    (Submodel~14).
    \item \textbf{The uncalibrated peer coefficients.} $q_{\text{base}}$, $\beta$ and the
    threshold parameters of Submodel~11 have no South African estimate. The
    $\beta = 0$ and $\gamma = 0$ control arms are reported beside every peer-influence result;
    the level-sensitivity arms run at $\beta = 0$ only.
    \item \textbf{The rolling limit.} No South African provider publishes a limit. We set
    $\lambda = 0.10$ so that four platform limits sum to about Woolard's illustrative
    \pounds1{,}000 of credit amassed across several lenders~\citep{woolard2021}, which is 39\% of United
    Kingdom median monthly equivalised household disposable income in the year to March 2020
    \citep{ons_income_fye2020}. We sweep $\lambda$ from 0.10 to 1.0 and read the sweep against a
    band of 0.10 to 0.48. The upper end is Afterpay's maximum of A\$2{,}000 as a share of
    Australian median monthly equivalised household disposable income in 2019--20
    \citep{asic2020rep672,abs_income_2019_20}. The Australian scale puts a multi-member
    household's equivalised income below its income, so that share overstates the limit against
    the household income $\lambda$ multiplies (Submodel~12). The United Kingdom scale sets a
    couple's equivalised income equal to its income, so the lower end is closer to like for like.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Design Rationale: Alternatives Considered and Rejected}
\label{app:rationale}

Chapter~\ref{ch:design} states the rules the model uses. This appendix records the alternatives
that were considered and rejected for each, and why. It is kept out of the body because the
rejections are not needed to understand the model, and kept in the thesis because they are needed
to defend it.

\section{Population Construction}

\textbf{Statistical fusion of \ac{NIDS} and \ac{FinScope}, rejected in favour of a cell-donor
match.} A hot-deck or model-based fusion would recover finer joint structure between the survey
instruments than copying flags within an income-quintile-by-province cell. It would also require
its own validation, introduce a real risk of fitting the idiosyncrasies of two surveys instead of
the population behind them, and take on a calibration burden disproportionate to the four
categorical flags actually being imported. The crude match reproduces cell marginals, which is
what the model needs, and its diagnostics are reported in Section~\ref{sec:match}.

\textbf{\ac{CPI} forwarding to 2022, rejected in favour of 2017-only units.} An earlier design
forwarded \ac{NIDS} monetary values to 2022. This required a second reference year, assumptions
about how relative incomes moved between the two, and 2022-vintage validation targets. Working
entirely in 2017 Rands removes all three, and the 2017 anchor is independently required by the
counterfactual injection design of Section~\ref{sec:injection}.

\section{Household Decision Rules}

\textbf{A propensity-to-consume or buffer-stock consumption rule (Submodel~2), rejected.} Both
require a calibrated propensity for which no South African estimate exists, and both would discard
the committed-versus-discretionary expenditure split already observed in the survey data. The
two-tier rule uses what the data records instead of imposing what it does not.

\textbf{Cost ranking as the lender-choice rule (Submodel~5), rejected.} \ac{BNPL} is nominally
interest-free, so a cost-minimising rule selects it trivially and attributes the choice to price.
The evidence attributes the choice to convenience and social approval \cite{ackert2025bnpl}, and
the distinction matters because the two mechanisms imply different interventions: a price-driven
choice would respond to disclosure of effective cost, while a norm-driven one would not.

\textbf{Scheduled amortisation as the sole repayment rule (Submodel~6), rejected.} Keys and Wang
\cite{Keys2019} find that 29 percent of credit-card accounts pay at or near the contractual
minimum, and that near-minimum payers shift their payments when the formula changes, which is the
signature of anchoring, not of planned amortisation. A uniform amortisation rule would
suppress precisely the behaviour by which obligations persist and compound.

\textbf{A debt-service-to-income threshold as the default condition (Submodel~7), rejected.} Any
such threshold is arbitrary, and the \ac{NCA} itself prescribes none. Madeira's cash-flow
insolvency test --- default on the failure to finance minimum consumption --- is cited, consistent
with the affordability rule the model already implements, and validated against observed default
rates in a middle-income economy \cite{madeira2018chile}.

\textbf{A continuous present-bias parameter (Submodel~8), rejected.} No South African distribution
exists against which to calibrate one, so it would add a free parameter with no discipline. The
model carries a single binary behavioural type instead, whose share is sourced and swept.

\section{Lender and Platform Rules}

\textbf{A flat \ac{DSTI} cap as the credit-granting gate (Submodel~9), rejected.} The earlier
version of this model used a ceiling of 0.65. It was unsourced, and the \ac{NCA} prescribes no
debt-service-to-income ratio at all. It was also badly over-permissive at the bottom of the
distribution: a 65 percent rule would permit a household earning R900 per month to service R585
per month. The Regulation 23A residual-income test is what the regulation actually prescribes, and
it produces an income-varying ceiling from 10.4 percent to 83.2 percent of income.

\textbf{Making the \ac{BNPL} eligibility screen a real affordability test (Submodel~12),
rejected for the baseline.} The asymmetry between the Regulation 23A test that binds registered
lenders and the light automated screen that \ac{BNPL} providers apply is the mechanism under
study, not an inconvenience to be smoothed away. Imposing a genuine affordability test on
\ac{BNPL} is an \emph{intervention}, and it appears as the second lever of Submodel~15.

\textbf{A purely multiplicative peer-influence rule (Submodel~11), rejected.} Every household
opens with no \ac{BNPL} balance, so the reference-group adoption share is identically zero at
initialisation. Under a multiplicative rule, adoption could never begin. The additive form of
Equation~\ref{eq:peer} gives $q_{\text{base}}$ and $\beta$ the roles that $p$ and $q$ play in Bass
diffusion.

\section{Environment and Scheduling}

\textbf{Macroeconomic dynamics (Submodel~16), rejected.} Both Cardaci
\cite{cardaci2018inequality} and Madeira \cite{madeira2018chile} carry macro dynamics, because
both ask macro questions. Any time-variation here would confound the effect of injecting \ac{BNPL}
with a macro effect, making it impossible to attribute a change in default to the injection. The
divergence follows from asking a product-level question over 24 months, and its cost is stated in
Section~\ref{sec:lim-structural}.

\textbf{Uniform activation order (Submodel~17), rejected.} Credit in this model is scarce, being
rationed by the affordability gate and by per-platform limits. A fixed activation order would hand
the same households first claim on that scarce resource in every tick, manufacturing a persistent
artefactual advantage. Comer and Loerch \cite{comer2013activation} find statistically significant
differences in emergent population behaviour across activation regimes in a replicated model, and
Alizadeh and Cioffi-Revilla \cite{alizadeh2015activation} find that no scheme dominates, so the
choice is reported and tested instead of assumed away.

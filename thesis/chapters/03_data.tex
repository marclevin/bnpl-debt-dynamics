\chapter{Data and Population Construction}
\label{ch:data}

The model runs over 5{,}000 household agents, each carried from a real South African survey
household, not a draw from an assumed distribution. This chapter describes how that
population was built and what its validation does and does not establish.

\section{Design Principles}
\label{sec:data-principles}

Five commitments govern the construction, and each exists to remove a class of assumption that
would otherwise have to be defended.

\textbf{One reference year.} Everything is in 2017 Rands. There is no inflation forwarding and no
second monetary reference year anywhere in the data layer or the model. An earlier design forwarded
\ac{NIDS} values to 2022 using \ac{CPI}, which introduced a second set of prices, a set of
distributional assumptions about how relative incomes moved between the two years, and a set of
2022-vintage validation targets. Staying in 2017 removes all three at once, and it is also what
makes the counterfactual injection of Section~\ref{sec:injection} interpretable.

\textbf{One backbone source.} \ac{NIDS} Wave 5, fielded in 2017, supplies income, expenditure,
assets, debt, household composition, head demographics and survey weights. Every monetary quantity
in the model traces to a single survey instrument.

\textbf{One donor source.} \ac{FinScope} 2019 supplies four financial-inclusion flags and nothing
else.

\textbf{A simple match, not a statistical fusion.} Flags are transferred by copying them from a
randomly chosen donor within a matching cell. The alternative, a full statistical fusion, buys
finer joint structure at the price of a validation burden this thesis cannot discharge and a real
risk of fitting the quirks of two surveys instead of the population behind them.

\textbf{Validation is internal at this stage.} The population is checked against the survey
distributions it was drawn from and against \ac{FinScope} marginals. It is not, and cannot be,
validated behaviourally, because it has no behaviour yet. External behavioural targets attach to
the model in Chapter~\ref{ch:design}, and the distinction matters: passing every check in this
chapter establishes that the population is a faithful sample, and says nothing about whether the
model built on it behaves realistically.

\section{The Backbone: NIDS Wave 5}
\label{sec:backbone}

\ac{NIDS} Wave 5 contains 13{,}719 household records. After dropping records with missing or
unusable income, expenditure or weight fields, \textbf{10{,}841 valid households} remain,
representing a weighted population of approximately 18.67 million households.

Six quantities are derived per household.

\textbf{Monthly income} is the \ac{NIDS} derived household income aggregate. \textbf{Income
source} is assigned by comparing the wage, government-grant, remittance and other components and
taking the dominant one, giving each household one of \textsc{wage}, \textsc{grant} or
\textsc{other}. The distinction matters for the model, because Submodel~1 applies labour-income
shocks only to wage-dominant households: South African social grants are statutory transfers and
do not vary with employment.

\textbf{Committed expenditure} is food plus rent, and is treated downstream as an incompressible
floor. \textbf{Discretionary expenditure} is non-food expenditure less rent, floored at zero.
Splitting observed expenditure this way, instead of imposing a propensity-to-consume rule, keeps
the two-tier consumption structure of Submodel~2 anchored to something the survey actually
measured.

\textbf{Liquid savings} proxies the cash buffer using the \ac{NIDS} financial-assets field,
winsorised at the 99th percentile (R133{,}662). This is the weakest field in the backbone. It is a
household's reported financial assets, not a demand-deposit balance, and it is the least reliably
reported quantity in the survey. Section~\ref{sec:lim-savings} returns to the consequences, which
are not trivial, since one of the model's four validation patterns is defined on it.

\textbf{Consolidated traditional debt} is the \ac{NIDS} financial-debt field.

Households are then assigned to \textbf{per-capita income quintiles}, weighted by survey weight.
The resulting bounds are R900, R1{,}801, R3{,}400 and R7{,}712 per person per month. Weighted
quintile shares fall between 19.6 and 20.4 percent, as they must. Head-of-household demographics
--- age, gender, race and education --- were joined from the \ac{NIDS} individual-derived file
through the household roster, matching 10{,}736 of 10{,}841 households, or 99 percent. The
education gradient across quintiles is textbook, running from 4 percent tertiary attainment in Q1
to 54 percent in Q5.

\section{The Donor Match: FinScope 2019}
\label{sec:match}

\ac{NIDS} does not record whether a household is banked, holds formal credit, holds a savings
product, or uses informal finance. \ac{FinScope} does, and those four flags gate behaviour in the
model: Submodel~12 makes \ac{BNPL} eligibility conditional on banked status, which caps \ac{BNPL}
access at 83.1 percent of the population by an observed field, not by a free parameter.

The four flags were resolved to \ac{FinScope} question codes --- F1 for banked status, G5 and
G10--G14 for formal credit access, K7 for savings product holding, and the informal-finance
battery --- and transferred by a \textbf{cell-donor match}. Each \ac{NIDS} household is assigned
a cell defined by per-capita income quintile crossed with province, a randomly chosen
\ac{FinScope} respondent from the same cell is drawn, and that respondent's four flags are copied
across. Forty-five cells result, every one of them carrying at least 30 donors, so
\textbf{no household required a fallback draw} to a coarser cell.

The match is deliberately crude and reproduces cell marginals but not finer joint structure.
Its diagnostics are the differences between synthetic and \ac{FinScope} national rates: 0.4
percentage points for banked status, 2.3 for formal credit access, 0.1 for informal finance and
1.8 for savings product holding. The largest by-quintile discrepancy across all twenty
flag-by-quintile combinations is 3.0 percentage points.

Two features of this cell are worth noting, because it does more work than a matching key
normally would. It is also the reporting stratum for quintile-level results, and it is the
reference group through which peer influence operates in Submodel~11. All three uses rest on the
same claim, that income and geography together identify households in comparable circumstances,
so using one construct for all three is a deliberate economy.

\ac{FinScope} 2019 is used as a proxy for the 2017 financial-inclusion landscape, because no 2017
wave is available. The imported variables are categorical and therefore need no deflation, but the
two-year gap is a real limitation and is recorded in Section~\ref{sec:lim-finscope}.

\section{Constructing Debt Servicing}
\label{sec:servicing}

Neither survey records what a household pays each month to service its debt, and the model cannot
run without it. Servicing is therefore constructed, in three steps.

First, each household's consolidated balance is amortised at the \textbf{\ac{NCA} statutory
maximum prescribed interest rate} for the relevant sub-sector, under the regime in force from 6
May 2016 and therefore valid for 2017, evaluated at the 2017 repurchase rate of 7.00 percent. This
gives 21 percent for credit facilities, 24 percent for other credit agreements and 28 percent for
unsecured credit transactions. A minimum term of six months is imposed, which prevents small
balances at short assumed terms from generating implausible instalments.

Second, the resulting instalment is capped by the \textbf{Regulation 23A residual-income test},
the same rule the lender applies in Submodel~9. An earlier version of this construction used a
flat \ac{DSTI} ceiling of 65 percent, which was unsourced. The \ac{NCA} prescribes no
debt-service-to-income ratio at all. It prescribes a table of minimum necessary monthly living
expenses by gross income band, and affordability is judged on what remains after those expenses
and all other committed obligations are subtracted. The ceiling this implies varies sharply with
income, from 10.4 percent of income at R900 per month to 83.2 percent at R7{,}712 per month. Both
worked examples published with the regulation are asserted in the construction notebook.

The cap binds for \textbf{158 households}, or 3.4 percent of the 4{,}702 households carrying debt.
That it binds rarely is the point: the affordability rule is meant to act as a backstop against
implausible instalments generated by the rate and term assumptions, and if it were doing most of
the work, the assumptions beneath it would be wrong.

Third, a diagnostic worth reporting in its own right. \textbf{Fifty-three households carrying debt
have gross income at or below the Regulation 23A minimum-expense norm for their income band.}
Their residual income is zero or negative before any instalment is considered, which means no
\ac{NCA}-compliant lender could lawfully have granted the debt they are recorded as holding. These
households were left in the population and reported as a finding. Three
readings are available and this thesis does not adjudicate between them: the debt may be informal
and outside the \ac{NCA} entirely, which the \ac{NIDS} debt field does not distinguish; the income
or debt figures may be misreported; or the credit may have been granted non-compliantly. Whichever
holds, removing them would be quietly deleting evidence about either the survey or the credit
market.

\section{Resampling to a Fixed Population}
\label{sec:resample}

The 10{,}841 weighted households are resampled with replacement, with probability proportional to
survey weight, to a fixed population of \textbf{5{,}000 agents} drawn from \textbf{3{,}250 unique
source households}, under seed 20240601. Weighting at this step is what makes the unweighted
5{,}000 agents behave like the weighted national population, at a fixed size that can be re-run
many times.

The resample introduces a small distortion, and it should be stated. The
weighted per-capita income Gini of the source population is 0.651; the unweighted Gini of the
5{,}000 agents is 0.671. The resample is slightly more unequal than the population it represents,
by 0.019 Gini points. Population-size stability was checked at 1{,}000, 5{,}000 and 10{,}000
agents.

Four observations about this scorecard, since a row of fourteen ticks invites more confidence than
it has earned.

\textbf{Check C is the only genuinely external one.} The per-capita income Gini of 0.651 falls
inside the 0.63 to 0.70 band reported for South Africa by the World Bank and Statistics South
Africa, and no step in the construction targeted it. The household-level Gini is 0.611. Every
other check in groups A, B and F asks whether the population reproduces the source it was drawn
from, which is a question about faithful sampling and not about external realism.

\textbf{Check D was circular until it was rewritten.} It originally asserted that the maximum
\ac{DSTI} in the population was at or below 0.66 --- which is to say, it tested the cap that the
construction had just imposed, and could not have failed. It now tests how often the affordability
guard has to bind, which is a real question about whether the rate and term assumptions beneath it
are sane. The episode is reported here instead of being quietly fixed, because a validation suite that
can only pass is worse than no validation suite.

\textbf{Two checks pass with very little margin.} The resample Gini gap of 0.019 sits just inside
its 0.02 tolerance, and mean household size at 3.03 sits 0.27 below the Statistics South Africa
benchmark of roughly 3.3 within a $\pm$0.5 tolerance. Neither fails; neither is comfortable.

\textbf{Nothing here validates behaviour.} The population is a faithful, internally consistent
sample of South African households in 2017 with plausible balance sheets. Whether households
built from it borrow, repay and default realistically is a question about the model, and it is
answered against the four external patterns registered in Section~\ref{sec:purpose}.

Two fields are absent by design. Every household opens with a \ac{BNPL} balance of zero, which is
the injection design of Section~\ref{sec:injection} and not a missing feature. And no household
carries a behavioural type from the data: the minimum-payer distinction of Submodel~6 is assigned
stochastically at initialisation, because no South African survey records repayment behaviour of
that kind.

\section{The Household Population}
\label{ch:population}

We construct a synthetic population of 5{,}000 household agents, derived directly from empirical South African survey microdata rather than assumed theoretical distributions. This section details the construction of this population framework, outlining the primary data sources, the derivation of household-level variables, the imputation of financial inclusion metrics, and the subsequent validation of the constructed population against external benchmarks. 

Establishing a representative baseline population is necessary to address the study's primary research questions. However, while this section validates the static structural characteristics of the population, the dynamic behavioural realism of these households is evaluated separately in Section~\ref{sec:results-baseline}. All monetary values are denominated in 2017 South African Rand (ZAR) to align with the primary dataset, with no inflation adjustments applied to the data layer.

\subsection{Primary Dataset: NIDS Wave 5}
\label{sec:backbone}

The core structural data is drawn from the National Income Dynamics Study (NIDS) Wave 5 \cite{nids_w5_2018}. The raw dataset comprises 13{,}719 household records. Following the removal of observations with missing or anomalous income, expenditure, or survey weight variables, a robust sample of 10{,}841 households is retained. Applied to the national weights, this sample represents a population of approximately 18.67 million South African households.

Six primary economic variables are derived for each household directly from the survey:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Monthly income:} The aggregated household income as derived in NIDS.
    \item \textbf{Income source:} Categorised by the dominant income stream (wage, government grant, or remittance/other). This distinction is necessary for Submodel~1, which isolates labour-income shocks to wage-reliant households, given the statutory stability of South African social grants.
    \item \textbf{Committed expenditure:} The sum of food and rent expenditures, establishing a minimum consumption floor.
    \item \textbf{Discretionary expenditure:} Total non-food expenditure exclusive of rent, floored at zero. Segmenting expenditure facilitates the two-tier consumption structure implemented in Submodel~2, grounding it in empirical household spending rather than an arbitrary propensity-to-consume parameter.
    \item \textbf{Liquid savings:} Proxied via the NIDS financial-assets field and winsorised at the 99th percentile (ZAR 133{,}662) to mitigate the influence of extreme outliers.
    \item \textbf{Traditional debt:} The consolidated financial-debt field as reported in NIDS.
\end{itemize}

Households are subsequently stratified into per-capita income quintiles, bounded at ZAR 900, ZAR 1{,}801, ZAR 3{,}400, and ZAR 7{,}712 per person per month. Demographic characteristics of the household head—specifically age, gender, race, and educational attainment—are joined from the NIDS individual-derived file, resulting in successful matches for 10{,}736 of the 10{,}841 households.

\subsection{Imputing Financial Inclusion: FinScope 2019}
\label{sec:match}

Because NIDS lacks granular data on financial product utilisation, four binary financial inclusion indicators—banked status, formal credit access, savings product ownership, and informal finance usage—are imputed using the FinScope 2019 survey \cite{finscope2019}. These indicators serve as gating parameters in the model; for instance, Submodel~12 restricts \ac{BNPL} eligibility to banked households, organically capping market access at the observed 83.1\% rather than relying on a free parameter.

Imputation is executed via a cell-donor matching algorithm. Each NIDS household is assigned to a stratum defined by the cross-tabulation of per-capita income quintile and province. A donor respondent is randomly drawn from the corresponding FinScope stratum, and the four inclusion flags are transferred. This process yields 45 distinct cells, each containing a minimum of 30 donors, ensuring no imputation requires fallback to a broader aggregation. While this methodology preserves cell marginals, minor deviations at the national level emerge: differences between the synthetic population and FinScope marginals are 0.4 percentage points for banked status, 2.3 for formal credit, 1.8 for savings products, and 0.1 for informal finance. The maximum observed discrepancy across all quintile-by-flag combinations is 3.0 percentage points.

Beyond imputation, these assigned cells establish the reporting strata for quintile-level analysis and define the reference networks driving peer-influence dynamics in Submodel~11.

\subsection{Debt Servicing Construction}
\label{sec:servicing}

As neither NIDS nor FinScope records the actual monthly cost of debt servicing, these obligations must be computationally estimated. This construction follows a three-step algorithmic approach grounded in South African regulatory frameworks:

First, consolidated debt balances are amortised using the statutory maximum interest rates prescribed by the National Credit Act (NCA) regulations effective 6 May 2016 \cite{sa_ncr_ratecaps_2016}. Evaluated against the 2017 repurchase rate of 7.00\%, these maximums are 21\% for credit facilities, 24\% for other credit agreements, and 28\% for unsecured credit. A minimum term of six months is applied to prevent small balances from generating mathematically inflated, implausible monthly instalments.

Second, the resulting instalment is constrained by the NCA Regulation 23A residual-income test, mirroring the affordability assessments executed by lenders in Submodel~9. The resultant ceiling scales with income, ranging from 10.4\% at the ZAR 900 threshold to 83.2\% at ZAR 7{,}712. This regulatory cap binds for only 3.4\% (158 households) of the 4{,}702 indebted households, indicating that the baseline amortisation assumptions do not systematically overestimate servicing costs.

Finally, the data reveals 53 indebted households whose gross income falls below the Regulation 23A minimum-expense norm, meaning compliant lenders could not legally extend this credit. These observations are retained in the model, as the underlying data cannot definitively distinguish between survey misreporting, non-compliant formal lending, or unrecorded informal debt arrangements.

\subsection{Population Resampling}
\label{sec:resample}

To generate a computationally tractable and consistent ABM environment, the 10{,}841 weighted NIDS households are resampled with replacement to construct a fixed population of 5{,}000 agents. This procedure, executed with probability proportional to the survey weight (seed: 20240601), yields a population comprising 3{,}250 unique source households. While this step allows the unweighted agent population to mimic national distributions, the resampling process introduces a marginal distortion in inequality: the per-capita income Gini coefficient shifts from 0.651 in the weighted source to 0.671 in the unweighted agent population.

It is important to qualify the scope of this validation. With the exception of the Gini coefficient benchmark (Check C), which successfully aligns with the established 0.63 to 0.70 range for South Africa \cite{worldbank_gini_zaf}, these diagnostics primarily confirm sample fidelity rather than absolute real-world accuracy. The validation demonstrates that the synthetic population accurately reflects the underlying surveys, without introducing significant sampling errors. Establishing whether these agents engage in realistic borrowing, repayment, and default behaviours requires validation against out-of-sample dynamic targets, which is conducted in Section~\ref{sec:results-baseline}.

Notably, two dimensions of the model are absent from the underlying data by design. First, every agent is initialised with a \ac{BNPL} balance of zero, acting as the controlled system injection detailed in Section~\ref{sec:purpose}. Second, because empirical surveys do not systematically record individual psychological repayment heuristics, specific repayment behaviour profiles (such as the minimum-payer archetype outlined in Submodel~6) are assigned stochastically during model initialisation.

\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

% 250 words (SOL_PLAN_REFORMAT.md). Every number is read from a generated table:
% 14.2% from tab:ccmr, 45% from tab:stacking, 1.2 and 2.1 points from tab:baseline-arms.
South African households carried substantial credit distress before \ac{BNPL} arrived: 14.2\% of unsecured and credit-facility accounts were more than ninety days in arrears in 2017, under a regime of statutory affordability tests and bureau reporting from which \ac{BNPL} was exempt.
The reporting requirement now being implemented brings \ac{BNPL} inside that regime before any evidence of what it will do.
This thesis builds an agent-based model of 5{,}000 households constructed from \ac{NIDS} and \ac{FinScope} microdata, calibrates the traditional credit market to the 2017 arrears profile with \ac{BNPL} disabled, and then injects \ac{BNPL} into the same environment as a controlled counterfactual, with platforms that cannot see one another and a bureau that cannot see them.
Two parameters are fitted; the checks the model was not fitted to are reported with their failures.
Concurrent-facility stacking emerges without being programmed, 45\% of \ac{BNPL} holders across four platforms, yet population default does not rise with the number of platforms: the injection raises default by 1.2 percentage points without peer influence and 2.1 with it, however many firms the obligations are spread across.
Default rises smoothly with \ac{BNPL} access under both a linear and a heterogeneous-threshold adoption rule, with no tipping point even where adoption cascades.
And in the affordability channel of the reporting requirement, the only channel the model represents, neither bureau visibility nor mandatory affordability screening changes default, with or without peer adoption, because each acts on a single agreement and neither changes how many a household services at once.

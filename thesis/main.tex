\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

% Every number is read from a generated table or results/summary/results_numbers.json:
% 0.1 and 0.6 points from Section 4.1 (beta0/beta1_effect_pooled);
% purchase size and volume both matched: kappa0.28 and base_income at beta = 0 in tab:effect;
% single-tick shock and committed_relief rows of tab:effect;
% a sixth and 0.15 to 0.22 points from tab:scenarios.
This thesis asks how \ac{BNPL} changes household credit distress in South Africa, and what
bureau reporting, reportedly due from 2027, or affordability screening would change.
We build an agent-based model of 5{,}000 households from \ac{NIDS} and \acs{FinScope} microdata, fit it to the 2017 arrears profile without \ac{BNPL}, then add \ac{BNPL} platforms that cannot see one another and a bureau that cannot see them.
A household defaults after 98 days of unmet food, rent or debt payments.
The model reproduces household size, income inequality and the order of magnitude of debt, but not the intermediate arrears bands, the income profile of debt or \ac{BNPL} purchase size, so we read its results only as two-year comparisons between simulated scenarios.
Owing several platforms at once is common, at a rate set mainly by an assumed rule for choosing platforms; default does not change detectably with that rule or the platform count.
Adding \ac{BNPL} raises default by about 0.1 percentage points without peer influence and by 0.6 points with illustrative peer influence, under which borrowing is ten times higher and above one provider's reported range.
The effect grows with how often households borrow. It is not detectable in the two settings whose purchase size and volume both match provider data, and it disappears if income losses last one fortnight or if borrowing for food and rent is not counted as distress.
The model has no credit score, so reporting is tested only through the traditional lender's affordability assessment. Adding \ac{BNPL} obligations to it raises refusals by about a sixth and default by 0.15 to 0.22 points, most in the poorest quintile, because a refused household has no other credit.
Screening reduces \ac{BNPL} volume by 1 to 4\% and does not change default detectably.

\section*{Data and Code}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Data and Code}

% TODO (front matter): confirm whether the department requires a separate ethics

This study is a secondary analysis of licensed survey microdata. \ac{NIDS} Wave 5
is produced by the Southern Africa Labour and Development Research Unit at the
University of Cape Town and distributed by DataFirst \cite{nids_w5_2018}; the 2019
\ac{FinScope} is produced by FinMark Trust \cite{finscope2019}. Both are used under
their access conditions, and no respondent is identifiable in the data or the
results. The simulation code, the constructed population and the scripts that
generate every table and figure are submitted with this thesis.

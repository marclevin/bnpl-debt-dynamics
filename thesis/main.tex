\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

% Every number is read from a generated table or a results/summary JSON:
% 1.0 point: beta0_effect_pooled (1.03); baseline 14.4%: default during the run, tab:baseline-arms;
% 2.4 times: volume_ratio_peer_over_beta0; flat in beta: results/summary/beta_sweep.json;
% shortfall path: no-shortfall-BNPL row of tab:effect; households below food and rent:
% defaulter_credit.json and contestable_default.json (Section 3.5);
% about 13% (+124/954) and 0.20 points from tab:scenarios; 0.9 to 1.0 points: screening rows of tab:scenarios.
This thesis asks how \ac{BNPL} changes credit distress in a model of South African households, and what
bureau visibility and affordability screening would change. Bureau reporting of \ac{BNPL} is
reportedly due from 2027.
We build an agent-based model of 5{,}000 households from \ac{NIDS} and \acs{FinScope} microdata. A traditional lender applies the Regulation 23A test to the debts the bureau records, while four \ac{BNPL} platforms apply a light screen and cannot see one another. Hires follow labour-force data and job losses offset them in expectation.
The baseline starts from the 2017 arrears profile, and two parameters are fitted to its five bands before \ac{BNPL} is added.
A household defaults when an instalment on any facility is more than 90 days past due.
The model matches household size, income inequality and the order of magnitude of debt, and holds the arrears profile for two years, but the profile is calibrated rather than validated, so we interpret only differences between scenarios.
Many users owe several platforms at once, but default does not rise with the number of platforms.
From a baseline in which 14.4\% of households default over two years, adding \ac{BNPL} raises default by 1.0 percentage point, and the effect does not change with peer influence over the range tested, although households borrow 2.4 times as much at its top.
The effect runs through households that use \ac{BNPL} to bridge cash shortfalls and then fall more than 90 days behind on it, most of them households whose surveyed income does not cover food and rent; it disappears when \ac{BNPL} cannot be used against a shortfall.
The model has no credit score, so reporting acts through the lender's Regulation 23A test. Adding \ac{BNPL} obligations to it raises refusals by about 13\% and default by 0.20 points without peer influence, because in the model refused households have no other credit.
Screening \ac{BNPL} requests with the same test lowers default by 0.9 to 1.0 points, almost all in the poorest quintile.

\section*{Data and Code}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Data and Code}

% TODO (front matter): confirm whether the department requires a separate ethics
% statement for secondary analysis of licensed survey microdata, and the required
% wording for the data acknowledgements. Blocked on the departmental template.

This study is a secondary analysis of licensed survey microdata. \ac{NIDS} Wave 5
is produced by the Southern Africa Labour and Development Research Unit at the
University of Cape Town and distributed by DataFirst~\citep{nids_w5_2018}; the 2019
\ac{FinScope} is produced by FinMark Trust~\citep{finscope2019}. Both are used under
their access conditions, and no respondent is identifiable in the data or the
results. The simulation code, the constructed population and the scripts that
generate every table and figure are submitted with this thesis. The simulations were run in
Python 3.14.4 with Mesa 3.5.1; \texttt{simulation/requirements.txt} pins every package
version. The results are those of the commit tagged in the code release.

\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

% bands and the two fitted parameters (payment friction, arrears persistence): tab:arrears-fit,
% Section 3.4; validation misses: Section 3.5 (debt service: tab:pop-external; the book halves:
% 2,237 to 1,175, Section 3.4; interest: tab:bnpl-on-checks; default has no external counterpart).
% Two-year horizon and "enter default during the run": Section 3.1 (sec:sim-params).
% 1.0 point: beta0_effect_pooled (1.03); 14.4%: default during the run without BNPL, 14.37,
% tab:baseline-arms; households below food and rent: short_group.json and
% short_group_lending.json (Sections 3.5 and 4.1); not detectable without the shortfall path:
% no-shortfall-BNPL row of tab:effect; no detectable slope:
% results/summary/beta_sweep.json; 0.20 points: scen_bureau_beta0_default, tab:scenarios (refusals
% +124/954); 0.9 to 1.0 points: screening rows of tab:scenarios; food and rent:
% tab:scenarios-distress. Bureau visibility with peer influence: 0.10 +/- 0.06, difference
% -0.09 +/- 0.08 (Section 5). Possibly understated: for 68% of the group's respondents a single
% income question covers food and rent (one_shot_covers_share_of_answered, short_group.json,
% Section 3.5). Peer influence raises borrowing: Sections 4.2 and 4.4.
South African households were in credit distress before \ac{BNPL} spread, and \ac{BNPL} agreements
do not appear on consumer credit profiles. Using an agent-based model of 5{,}000 households built
from South African survey microdata, we ask how \ac{BNPL} changes credit distress and what bureau
visibility and affordability screening would change. The model's traditional lender tests
affordability on the debts the bureau records, and four \ac{BNPL} platforms cannot see one
another's balances. Runs start from the 2017 arrears profile, to whose five bands we fit two
repayment parameters, and last two years. A household defaults when an instalment on any of its
credit, \ac{BNPL} included, is more than 90 days past due. Enabling \ac{BNPL} raises the share of
households that default during the run by 1.0 percentage point, from 14.4\%. The rise comes mostly
from households the lender refuses and the platforms accept, whose surveyed income, possibly
understated, does not cover food and rent. It is not detectable when \ac{BNPL} cannot be used
against a cash shortfall. Peer influence raises \ac{BNPL} borrowing without detectably changing the
effect. Without peer influence, bureau visibility raises default by 0.20
points, because the lender refuses more households, which in the model have no other credit.
Screening \ac{BNPL} with the same test lowers default by 0.9 to 1.0 points. Both policies leave
more households short of food and rent, screening only without peer influence. The fitted profile cannot
validate the model, opening debt service exceeds what households report, the number of indebted
households halves over the run, traditional interest does not rise with \ac{BNPL} as in United
States data, and no external figure measures default. We therefore interpret mainly differences
between runs, conditional on their shared assumptions.

\section*{Data and Code}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Data and Code}

% TODO (front matter): confirm whether the department requires a separate ethics
% statement for secondary analysis of licensed survey microdata, and the required
% wording for the data acknowledgements. Blocked on the departmental template.

This study is a secondary analysis of licensed survey microdata. \ac{NIDS} Wave 5
is produced by the Southern Africa Labour and Development Research Unit at the
University of Cape Town and distributed by DataFirst~\citep{nids_w5_2018}; the 2019
\ac{FinScope} is produced by FinMark Trust~\citep{finscope2019}. Both are used under
their access conditions, and no respondent is identifiable in the data or the
results. The simulation code, the constructed population and the scripts that
generate every table and figure are submitted with this thesis. The simulations were run in
Python 3.14.4 with Mesa 3.5.1; \texttt{simulation/requirements.txt} pins every package
version. The results come from the commit tagged in the code release.

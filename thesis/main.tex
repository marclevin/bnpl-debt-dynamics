\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

% Every number is read from a generated table, a results/summary JSON or Section 3:
% 5,000 households and four platforms: Section 3.1 and tab:experiments; the 2017 profile, its five
% bands and the two fitted parameters (payment friction, arrears persistence): tab:arrears-fit,
% Section 3.4; validation misses: Section 3.5 (debt service: tab:pop-external; the book halves:
% 2,237 to 1,175, Section 3.4; interest: tab:bnpl-on-checks; default has no external counterpart).
% Two-year horizon and "enter default during the run": Section 3.1 (sec:sim-params).
% 1.1 points: split_redraw_all (1.13, redrawn population, the main estimate since 2026-11-25);
% 1.0: beta0_effect_pooled (1.03, fixed population); 14.4%: default during the run without BNPL, 14.37,
% tab:baseline-arms; 3.6% short from the start (182 of 5,000): short_group.json (Section 3.5);
% refused by the lender and accepted by the platforms: 0.92 of the 1.08 points from the 55 of 120
% the lender never lends to, short_group_lending.json (Section 4.1); other households: +0.17 +/-
% 0.05, split_redraw_other, results_numbers.json (redrawn population; +0.11 +/- 0.07 pooled over the
% fixed population's blocks, beta0_effect_pooled_contestable, contestable_default.json). About half under the wage-income screen: +0.50 +/- 0.09
% against +1.08 +/- 0.11 on the same seeds, split_screen_all and split_bench_all; 1.1 points with
% the redrawn population: +1.13 +/- 0.06, split_redraw_all (results_numbers.json and
% effect_split.json, tab:effect-split, Section 4.1). Not detectable without the shortfall path:
% no-shortfall-BNPL row of tab:effect; no detectable slope:
% results/summary/beta_sweep.json; 0.20 points: scen_bureau_beta0_default, tab:scenarios (refusals
% +124/954); 0.9 to 1.0 points: screening rows of tab:scenarios; food and rent:
% tab:scenarios-distress. Bureau visibility with peer influence: 0.10 +/- 0.06, difference
% -0.09 +/- 0.08 (Section 5). Possibly understated: for 68% of the group's respondents a single
% income question covers food and rent (one_shot_covers_share_of_answered, short_group.json,
% Section 3.5). Peer influence raises borrowing: Sections 4.2 and 4.4.
In an agent-based model of 5{,}000 households built from South African survey microdata, we ask
how \ac{BNPL} changes credit distress and what bureau visibility and affordability screening
change. The model's traditional lender tests affordability on the debts the bureau records, which
exclude \ac{BNPL}. Its four platforms apply no such test and cannot see one another's balances.
Runs last two years from the 2017 arrears profile, to which we fit two parameters. A household
defaults when an instalment on any credit, \ac{BNPL} included, is more than 90 days past
due. In the model \ac{BNPL} raises default mainly among the 3.6\% of households short from the
start, whose surveyed income, possibly understated, does not cover food and rent. The rise comes
mostly from those the lender refuses and the platforms accept. For other households it is small.
Across households, default rises by 1.1 percentage points, from 14.4\%, when each comparison
draws a new population, and by 1.0 in one fixed population. The rise is not
detectable when \ac{BNPL} cannot be used against a cash shortfall. About half of it remains when
the platforms lend only to households with wage income. Peer influence raises \ac{BNPL} borrowing without detectably
changing the rise. Without peer influence, bureau
visibility raises default by 0.20 points, since in the model a refused household has no other
source of credit. Screening \ac{BNPL} with the lender's test lowers default by 0.9 to 1.0 points.
Both leave more households short of food and rent, screening only without peer influence. The
fitted profile cannot validate the model, opening debt service exceeds what households report,
the credit-active book halves, traditional interest does not rise with \ac{BNPL}
as in United States data, and no external figure measures default. We therefore interpret mainly
differences between runs, conditional on their shared assumptions.

\section*{Data and Code}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Data and Code}

% TODO (front matter): confirm whether the department requires a separate ethics
% statement for secondary analysis of licensed survey microdata, and the required
% wording for the data acknowledgements. Blocked on the departmental template.

This study is a secondary analysis of licensed survey microdata. \ac{NIDS} Wave 5
is produced by the Southern Africa Labour and Development Research Unit at the
University of Cape Town and distributed by DataFirst~\citep{nids_w5_2018}; the 2019
\ac{FinScope} is produced by FinMark Trust~\citep{finscope2019}. Both are used under
their access conditions, and no respondent is identifiable in the data or the
results. The simulation code, the constructed population and the scripts that
generate every table and figure are submitted with this thesis. The simulations were run in
Python 3.14.4 with Mesa 3.5.1; \texttt{simulation/requirements.txt} pins every package
version. The results come from the commit tagged in the code release.
